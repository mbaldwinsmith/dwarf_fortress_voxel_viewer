# Independent workshop critic review

## Baseline — 0 of 33 pass

Pass threshold: **4.8/5 for every profile**. These are subjective visual-fidelity ratings, based on each individual native completed-sprite composite (including its decorative top row), 3D orbit capture and ground-level capture in `artifacts/workshop-review-baseline/`. The comparison uses a neutral native material control: construction tint/palette is removed from the model so legitimate material recolouring is not mistaken for a fidelity error.

The rubric weights composition/silhouette (2.0), native texture and colour (1.25), characteristic props (1.0), and coherent 3D volume/grounding (0.75). Inferred unseen backs/depth are allowed. A score of 4.8 permits small interpretation differences; it does not permit missing visible identity-defining props or replacing the native layout with generic furniture.

The baseline models are coherent themed interpretations, but none reproduces the original closely enough to pass. The repeated issue is loss of native prop placement, silhouettes and surface detail. Generic table texture is reused on most machinery, and several large forms are invented or moved.

## Guidance for the next iteration

User clarification: **“Tables with flat item textures on would be ok!”** Native
flat tools, supplies and small-item details on genuinely raised 3D counters/tables
are therefore accepted without penalty. They do not need individual 3D models.
Larger identity-defining machinery, vessels, frames and structures still need
coherent volume, grounding and their original placement. Baseline scores above
remain unchanged because the baseline omitted that native surface detail.

- Recover native prop layout and characteristic surfaces before adding generic geometric detail.
- Separate completed floor/background from structural counters and the coloured prop overlay. The opaque floor must not become one extruded workshop block.
- Treat painted overlay alpha as perspective artwork, not a literal physical footprint: barrels already show a top and front; shadows, tools and hanging material need different depth rules.
- Preserve disconnected props and their relative positions. The decorative top row should represent height above the footprint, not floating objects or an extra floor row.
- Keep genuine ground-level volume: closed sides/undersides, supported benches and real frame openings. A flat billboard replacement does not pass.
- Preserve native prop colours independently from construction material. Copper stills, metal anvils, wood barrels, cloth and tools must retain their own visual identities.

No source files were changed by the critic. Subsequent iterations will be added below after direct visual review; passing scores will not be inferred from tests or source code alone.

## Native-derived prototype sanity check (`native3`)

This was a representative check while known extraction problems were being fixed,
not a full 33-profile rerating. Native surface detail and placement improve, but
the sampled models do not yet pass:

- **Wood furnace:** the defining dome and chimney are almost entirely absent;
  extraction leaves a low grey wall behind the fire.
- **Carpenter:** native counter detail is promising. Tools need the approved flat
  treatment; bench/chest bodies need rectangular tops instead of striped rounded
  extrusions, and loose shavings/stools must not become thick lumps.
- **Metalsmith forge:** native placement is restored, but the anvil, bars and coal
  inflate into broad wedges instead of their characteristic individual forms.
- **Mechanic:** the machinery is one broad flattened slab with a striped roof;
  native vertical posts, gears and exposed axles need separated depth/height.
- **Still:** rear barrels become joined loaf-like blocks, and the copper vessel
  has a thick striped roof. Separate vessels, barrels, pipe and receiver.
- **Loom:** native front detail is recognisable, but the loom reads as a solid
  cabinet. It needs the real open space within its frame.
- **Screw press:** absent in both model captures; no rating is possible until it
  renders.

Opaque counter boxes, excessively tall counter items and stretched contour-pixel
side textures were also visible. The accepted flat item textures on real tables
remain a good approach; large machinery still requires coherent volume.

### Shared fixes before another full review

1. **Author horizontal counter regions explicitly.** The repeated vertical white walls and long floating rails across masonry, crafts, jewellery, bowyer, butcher, fishery, kitchen and soap maker are a systematic surface/orientation failure, not small prop differences. Depot's brown tables need the same treatment.
2. **Do not extrude an entire connected artwork cluster into a cuboid.** Farmer, clothier, ashery, dyer, tanner and furnace supplies visibly become picture walls with blank brown/grey roofs. Separate physical objects first; use cylindrical vessels, boxes, thin racks and flat accepted tool detail as appropriate.
3. **Separate front art from top UVs.** Native perspective sprites already contain both surfaces. Stretching their contour through depth creates long zebra roofs, and projecting whole sprites onto tops duplicates handles/axles. Use interior material crops for inferred sides/tops, retaining full native details only on matching surfaces.
4. **Check connectivity from ground view.** Floating millstone cap, staircase quern handle, broken screw-press supports, floating fire sheets and disconnected forge hearth are actual visual geometry defects. Every structural part needs a plausible junction/support.
5. **Retain native characteristic height.** Furnaces need rounded dome/arch and chimney, machinery needs upright shafts/frames. Increasing texture fidelity alone cannot compensate for flattening these structures.

All scores are below the requested threshold. No profile is marked passed merely because it improved relative to baseline. No source files were changed by this critic.
